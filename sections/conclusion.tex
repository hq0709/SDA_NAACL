\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

With a diagnostic that crosses useful-concept and distractor statedness, this study identifies the Statedness Bias in LLM memory admission: importance judgments favor passage words and discard implied useful concepts that the model recognizes. On Qwen3-8B, statedness-cued wording reverses this preference, and both that wording and statedness rules raise implied retention at the cost of stated retention. Evaluating admission therefore requires statedness-balanced candidate pools, with retention and answer accuracy reported separately for stated and implied episodes. The gap between recognizing a useful concept and admitting it motivates SDA, which trains the importance judgment on the model's own signals. SDA removes the strong form of the Statedness Bias and narrows the A-B gap from 54.1 to 25.0 points while improving admission of stated and implied useful concepts.

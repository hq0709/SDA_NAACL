\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This study identifies a Statedness Bias in LLM memory admission: importance judgments favor passage words and discard useful implied concepts that the model recognizes. On Qwen3-8B, statedness-cued wording reverses this preference, and both that wording and statedness rules raise implied retention at the cost of stated retention. The gap between recognizing a useful concept and admitting it motivates SDA, which trains the importance judgment on the model's own signals. SDA removes the strong form and narrows the gap between conditions A and B while improving admission of stated and implied useful concepts. Evaluating admission therefore requires statedness-balanced candidate pools, with retention and answer accuracy reported separately for stated and implied episodes.

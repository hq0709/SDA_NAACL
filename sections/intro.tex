\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

An LLM agent must choose what to remember before it knows the next
question, and the useful concept is often an inference that the passage
never states. A description of a landmark can identify a city without
naming it, so remembering that city preserves the link to a later
question about the local language. Once the passage leaves the context,
the agent's answer depends on which inferences survived admission to
memory.

Memory systems make this choice through importance judgments: the
model's yes/no importance report, its 1--10 importance rating
\citep{park2023generative}, or published admission criteria built on
context support \citep{amac2026,consistencygate2026}. In each case, the
passage provides the evidence for admission, but an agent also needs
to preserve what it inferred from the passage. A memory system can
therefore discard the useful concept even when the model has
identified it.

The importance report is the model's yes/no answer to whether a
candidate is among the most important things to remember, scored by
$P(\mathrm{yes})$ against $P(\mathrm{no})$. A candidate is stated when
the passage mentions it. Controlled pools fix each episode's useful
concept and vary which candidates are stated (\S\ref{sec:construction}).
Among unstated candidates, the report ranks the useful concept well,
with pooled area under the ROC curve (AUC) of 0.880--0.897 across six
models. On the original candidate pool, where the useful concept is
usually the only unstated candidate, its AUC falls below chance
(0.322--0.427). The 1--10 importance rating and published criteria
built on context support also favor stated candidates.

The workspace readout is a logit-lens score: the peak reciprocal rank
of a candidate when each layer's residual state is decoded into the
vocabulary \citep{nostalgebraist2020logitlens}. It serves as a proxy
for the candidate's presence in the model's workspace, the verbalizable
concepts the model is currently reasoning with
\citep{anthropic2026workspace}. On the same original pool, it ranks the
useful concept above chance, so the model's intermediate layers encode
information that its importance report does not express. Naming the
useful concept in an otherwise unchanged episode raises AUC for both
signals, with a larger increase for the workspace readout
(Figure~\ref{fig:hero}).

Self-Distilled Admission (SDA) turns these internal rankings into
admission decisions (Figure~\ref{fig:sda}). The frozen model is its own
teacher: it ranks each episode's candidates by workspace readout and
output likelihood (the candidate's mean token log-probability given the
passage) and labels the top two for admission. A low-rank adaptation
(LoRA) adapter \citep{hu2021lora} learns these labels through the
model's importance prompt.

SDA improves answer accuracy over existing admission scores. On the
statedness-balanced pool, in which the passage states the useful
concept in half the episodes, SDA exceeds each model's own importance
report and its 1--10 importance rating in all 18 comparisons across six
models and three memory budgets (Table~\ref{tab:balanced}). With two
stored concepts, Qwen3-8B gains 10.7 accuracy points over its
importance report and 5.2 over the rating. ConsistencyGate, A-MAC, and
Nemori fall below the untrained report; for Qwen3-8B with two stored
concepts, SDA exceeds these published admission criteria by 14.1--27.8
points (Table~\ref{tab:balancedbaselines}).

Removing the workspace readout from the teacher lowers retention of
unstated useful concepts in each of five models, by 6.6 points on
average with one stored concept (Table~\ref{tab:teacherretention}).
It also lowers mean answer accuracy on a paired-passage set, where two
episodes share the same candidates and only the passage identifies the
useful concept (Table~\ref{tab:crosspairpooled}).

Served through vLLM, the Qwen3-8B adapter scores 242 candidates per
second on the original pool, with answer accuracy (50.1\%) within 0.3
points of its offline selections (Table~\ref{tab:sdadeploy}).

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Importance judgments favor stated candidates.}
    Controlled pools separate statedness from usefulness: the
    importance report, the 1--10 rating, and criteria built on context
    support prefer stated candidates, while the workspace readout ranks
    unstated useful concepts above chance (\S\ref{sec:gap}).
    \item \textbf{Self-Distilled Admission.} SDA distills the workspace
    readout and output likelihood into a LoRA adapter queried through
    the model's importance prompt (\S\ref{sec:method}).
    \item \textbf{Gains over reports, ratings, and published criteria.}
    Evaluation across six models from three families, three memory
    budgets, and five candidate pools shows answer accuracy gains over
    the importance report, the rating, and published admission
    criteria. Ablations isolate each teacher signal: the workspace
    readout's contribution concentrates on unstated useful concepts,
    and both signals contribute to answer accuracy on the
    paired-passage set (\S\ref{sec:recall}, \S\ref{sec:ablations}).
\end{itemize}

External memory systems such as MemGPT, Mem0, and A-MEM let LLM agents retain information across interactions \citep{packer2023memgpt,mem0,amem2025}. Agents choose what to store under a budget before future questions arrive. Systems use judgments such as Generative Agents' 1--10 importance rating \citep{park2023generative} and admission criteria such as ConsistencyGate and A-MAC \citep{consistencygate2026,amac2026}. Does the judgment track what a passage means, or only what it states? A future question that combines a passage with world knowledge depends on what the passage implies.

In Figure~\ref{fig:episode}, Yara examines cuneiform tablets beside a river that fed early agriculture, but the passage never names Mesopotamia. A later question asks which modern country she visited. The yes/no importance report stores \texttt{silt} and \texttt{tablet}; the frozen reader cannot name a country. Storing \texttt{mesopotamia} and \texttt{tablet} instead lets it answer Iraq. Recovering \texttt{mesopotamia} requires a bridging inference \citep{haviland1974whatsnew}, linking the passage to prior knowledge. Unstated-first with report ordering, which stores unstated candidates first, keeps \texttt{mesopotamia} but adds \texttt{levant}, and the reader answers Lebanon.

Readers remember the situation a text describes, including inferences, more than its wording \citep{bransford1972sentence,kintsch1988construction}. Memory admission must likewise separate statedness, whether a passage states a candidate, from usefulness, whether a future question needs it. Here, \texttt{silt} is stated but useless; \texttt{mesopotamia} is useful but unstated, or implied. Naming \texttt{mesopotamia} changes its statedness without changing its usefulness.

We hypothesize a \textbf{Statedness Bias}: importance judgments favor concepts because the passage states them. The importance prompt asks for passage support, which passage words provide directly; published admission criteria score that support explicitly \citep{consistencygate2026,amac2026}. Conversational benchmarks such as LoCoMo and LongMemEval evaluate end-to-end retrieval and use of conversation content without separating stated from implied information \citep{maharana2024locomo,wu2025longmemeval}, so admission of implied concepts remains untested.

Our diagnostic crosses useful-concept and distractor statedness (\S\ref{sec:design}). Win rate is the share of within-episode comparisons in which the useful concept outranks a distractor, counting ties half. Comparing an implied useful concept against stated distractors (A) exposes the bias, whose strong form is a win rate below chance; comparing it against unstated distractors (B) tests recognition. We evaluate ranking and answer accuracy on a statedness-balanced pool, where statedness alone ranks at chance, and the original pool, where the useful concept is usually the only unstated candidate. All policies share candidate strings and a reader, so their answer differences arise from admission. The diagnostic applies to any importance judgment. We evaluate six models from three families (7B to 32B) on 363 test episodes.

The Mesopotamia example is representative. The report ranks implied useful concepts above stated distractors in 38.3\% of comparisons versus 92.4\% against unstated distractors (Table~\ref{tab:grid}). The rating also shows the Statedness Bias; ConsistencyGate and A-MAC utility favor stated candidates. On Qwen3-8B, statedness-cued wording trades stated for implied useful concepts. The model recognizes implied useful concepts; its logit-lens readout and output likelihood show weaker statedness preference. Self-Distilled Admission (SDA) trains the importance judgment on these signals through an admission-only LoRA adapter \citep{hu2021lora}, raising six-model mean implied and stated useful-concept retention with two stored concepts from 33.4\% to 63.8\% and 45.3\% to 80.3\%, respectively (Table~\ref{tab:splitfull}). It exceeds the report and rating in all 18 original-pool comparisons and gains 11.0 accuracy points over the report on Qwen3-8B's enlarged pool at $k=2$ (App.~\ref{app:poolresults}). The bias replicates on TwoHopFact, where SDA, applied without training on it, leads non-reference policies in answer accuracy at every budget (\S\ref{sec:twohop}).

Our contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item We identify the Statedness Bias: importance judgments favor passage words despite recognizing implied useful concepts.
\item We introduce a statedness diagnostic and SDA, trained on the model's own signals.
\item SDA removes the strong form of the Statedness Bias and exceeds the report and rating in all 18 model-budget comparisons on the statedness-balanced pool, with replication on public TwoHopFact.
\end{itemize}

\section{Diagnostic Design}\label{sec:design}

\paragraph{Episodes.}
In the Decoupled construction, GPT-4.1 writes a passage about a named person, plus the useful concept, a future question and its answer. The answer is absent from passage and candidates; the question connects to the passage only through the person's name. We evaluate 363 test and 316 held-out episodes. Training and validation use other constructions; separate development sets guide configuration (App.~\ref{app:training}).

\paragraph{Candidate pools.}
The original pool has 1,607 candidates, 4.43 per episode; the useful concept is the sole unstated candidate in 95\% of episodes, making unstated selection a shortcut. The main statedness-balanced pool removes this shortcut: in fixed test-set order, odd-position passages remain unchanged; even-position passages get ``The passage also mentions \emph{useful concept}.'' appended. Original candidates remain; odd and even positions add the first four and three unstated distractors, respectively, from the pool with unstated distractors. An LLM generates these distractors within the useful concept's semantic category; the passage neither states nor implies them. The pool has 190 stated and 173 implied episodes, 7.93 candidates per episode. Useful concepts are 13.3\% of stated and 12.0\% of unstated candidates; statedness alone ranks at chance (pooled AUC 0.515; within-episode AUC 0.516). Added candidates are negative; questions, answers, and utility labels stay fixed. Controls test the association between naming and usefulness (naming-balanced pool), selection among unstated candidates (pool with unstated distractors), more candidates (enlarged pool), passage-dependent usefulness with shared candidates (paired-passage set), and passage dependence with candidates, labels, and questions fixed (passage replacement; App.~\ref{app:pools}).

\paragraph{Quality checks.}
Rule-based filters and a blind alias filter check every episode (App.~\ref{app:benchmarks}). A blind semantic judge grades the frozen reader's answers; string-match agreement is 85.1\%. SDA exceeds the importance report under both graders (App.~\ref{app:judge}).

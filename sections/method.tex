\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

Self-Distilled Admission (SDA) distills the frozen model's candidate rankings into a LoRA adapter used only for admission requests (Figure~\ref{fig:sda}; Algorithm~\ref{alg:sda}).

\subsection{Admission Task}\label{sec:problem}

Admission stores $K\subseteq C$, $|K|\leq k$, from passage $x$ and ordered candidates $C=(c_1,\ldots,c_m)$ before future question $q$ and answer $a$ are known. When $q$ arrives, the frozen reader sees only $q$ and $K$. Every policy receives identical candidates and budget.

Each episode centers on a useful concept linking the passage to a future answer (Figure~\ref{fig:episode}).

The importance prompt $u(c,x)$ asks whether $c$ is one of the most important concepts to remember for possible future questions and requests a single yes or no (App.~\ref{app:prompts}); Qwen3 uses thinking-disabled prompts. Let $Y,N$ contain distinct first tokens of the case and leading-space forms of \emph{yes} and \emph{no}, respectively:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
s_\theta(c;x)={}&\log\!\sum_{t\in Y}p_\theta(t\mid u(c,x))\\
&-\log\!\sum_{t\in N}p_\theta(t\mid u(c,x)),\\
V_\theta(c;x)={}&\sigma\!\left(s_\theta(c;x)\right).
\end{split}
\label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
The untrained model's score $V$ is the importance report; SDA ranks by $s_\theta$ with its adapter enabled. App.~\ref{app:prompts} defines the 1--10 importance rating.

\subsection{Teacher Rankings}\label{sec:teacher}

The workspace comprises verbalizable concepts the model is currently reasoning with \citep{anthropic2026workspace}. The workspace readout decodes each layer's residual state with the logit lens \citep{nostalgebraist2020logitlens,anthropic2026workspace}. Let $h_\ell(x)$ be the residual state at the final context position after layer $\ell$, of $L$ layers. Final normalization $\mathrm{LN}$ and unembedding $W_U$ give logits $z_\ell(x)=W_U\,\mathrm{LN}(h_\ell(x))$; token ranks are $\mathrm{rank}_\ell(t)=1+|\{v:z_{\ell,v}>z_{\ell,t}\}|$. The workspace readout is the peak reciprocal rank:
\begin{equation}
W(c;x)=\max_{\substack{1\leq\ell\leq L\\ t\in F(c)}}
\frac{1}{\mathrm{rank}_\ell(t)}.
\label{eq:wrr}
\end{equation}
$F(c)$ contains distinct first tokens of the candidate's bare and leading-space forms, each in original and capitalized form. One forward pass over $x$ supplies the required states. Output likelihood is $\mathrm{LL}(c;x)=\log p_{\mathcal M}(c\mid x)/|c|$, where $|c|$ is token length.

Both signals become mid-rank percentiles within the episode; ties receive their mean rank (App.~\ref{app:training}). The combined teacher averages these percentiles:
\begin{equation}
T(c;x)=\tfrac12\bigl(P_W(c;x)+P_{\mathrm{LL}}(c;x)\bigr).
\label{eq:teacher}
\end{equation}
Here $P_S$ denotes the percentile under score $S$. The two highest-ranked candidates receive $\tilde y=1$ for \emph{yes}; all others receive $\tilde y=0$ for \emph{no}. Candidate order breaks ties.

\subsection{Admission Distillation}\label{sec:student}

A LoRA adapter \citep{hu2021lora} on the frozen model learns the teacher's labels by answering the importance prompt. Cross-entropy trains target-token prediction while base parameters remain frozen:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L(\theta)=-\frac{1}{|\mathcal D|}
\sum_{(x,c)\in\mathcal D}
\log p_\theta\!\left(\tau(\tilde y(c;x))\mid u(c,x)\right).
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
The examples in $\mathcal D$ pair each passage $x$ with each candidate $c$ and its teacher label $\tilde y(c;x)$. The mapping $\tau$ assigns \emph{yes} to positive labels and \emph{no} to negative labels. Target probabilities are normalized over the vocabulary; prompt tokens are masked from the loss (App.~\ref{app:configuration}).

Training uses a rank-16 adapter for two epochs and selects the earliest checkpoint with the highest within-episode AUC on the in-distribution validation split. Optimization uses teacher labels; validation uses gold utility labels. The combined teacher was selected by mean answer accuracy across development sets and training runs.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Self-Distilled Admission.}
\label{alg:sda}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require frozen model $\mathcal M$; training episodes $\mathcal E$, each a passage $x$ with candidates $C$
\For{each episode $(x,C)\in\mathcal E$}
  \For{each candidate $c\in C$}
    \State $W(c;x)\gets$ peak reciprocal rank across layers \Comment{Eq.~\eqref{eq:wrr}}
    \State $\mathrm{LL}(c;x)\gets\log p_{\mathcal M}(c\mid x)/|c|$
  \EndFor
  \State $T(c;x)\gets\tfrac12\bigl(P_W(c;x)+P_{\mathrm{LL}}(c;x)\bigr)$ for all $c\in C$ \Comment{Eq.~\eqref{eq:teacher}}
  \State $\tilde y(c;x)\gets 1$ for the two highest $T$, else $0$
\EndFor
\State train adapter $\theta$ on $\mathcal L(\theta)$ over all $(x,c,\tilde y)$ \Comment{Eq.~\eqref{eq:loss}}
\State select the earliest checkpoint with the highest validation within-episode AUC
\Statex \textbf{Deployment:} given a passage $x$, candidates $C$ and budget $k$
\State store the $k$ candidates with the highest $s_\theta(c;x)$ \Comment{Eq.~\eqref{eq:score}}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Serving}\label{sec:serving}

SDA makes one yes/no forward pass per candidate, reading the importance prompt's output probabilities; the host stores the top-$k$ candidates, with candidate order breaking ties. The adapter is attached only to admission requests in a standard serving engine such as vLLM \citep{kwon2023pagedattention}, prefix caching reuses the shared passage, and all other requests, including the frozen reader's answers, use the unmodified model.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

We present the statedness diagnostic (\S\ref{sec:design}) and Self-Distilled Admission (SDA; \S\ref{sec:sda}).

\subsection{Statedness Diagnostic}\label{sec:design}

\paragraph{Episodes.}
GPT-4.1 builds Decoupled episodes: passages about named people, useful concepts, future questions and answers. Answers are absent from passages and candidates; questions connect to passages only through the person's name. We evaluate 363 test and 316 held-out episodes, checked by rule-based filters and a blind alias filter (App.~\ref{app:benchmarks}). Training and validation use Explicit and implied-answer constructions; separate development sets guide configuration (App.~\ref{app:training}).

Condition A compares an implied useful concept with a stated distractor: usefulness and statedness favor different candidates. It tests whether passage words displace useful concepts.

Condition B compares an implied useful concept with an unstated distractor, testing usefulness discrimination at fixed statedness. A and B share implied useful concepts, so A below B identifies the Statedness Bias.

Condition C compares a stated useful concept with a stated distractor, testing usefulness discrimination among passage words.

Condition D compares a stated useful concept with an unstated distractor. This sanity check aligns usefulness and statedness.

\paragraph{Candidate pools.}
The original pool contains 1,607 candidates, 4.43 per episode. In 95\% of episodes, the useful concept is the sole unstated candidate, making statedness a shortcut to usefulness.

The main statedness-balanced pool removes this shortcut. An LLM generates same-category distractors absent from and not implied by the passage, forming the pool with unstated distractors. In fixed test-set order, odd-position episodes retain their passage; even-position episodes append ``The passage also mentions \emph{useful concept}.'' All original candidates remain. Odd-position episodes add the first four unstated distractors from the pool with unstated distractors; even-position episodes add the first three. Added candidates are negative; questions, answers and original utility labels stay fixed.

The pool contains 190 stated and 173 implied episodes, with 7.93 candidates per episode. Useful concepts constitute 13.3\% of stated and 12.0\% of unstated candidates. Statedness alone ranks at chance: pooled AUC 0.515 and within-episode AUC 0.516.

The useful-concept-named control, naming-balanced pool, pool with unstated distractors, enlarged pool, paired-passage set and passage replacement test naming, candidate composition and passage dependence (App.~\ref{app:pools}).

\subsection{Self-Distilled Admission}\label{sec:sda}

SDA assumes that the bias lies in the importance judgment while the model recognizes implied useful concepts. Condition B and internal-signal measurements test this premise (\S\ref{sec:bias}; \S\ref{sec:knowledge}). A LoRA adapter learns labels from the model's own signals through the importance prompt and serves only admission (Figure~\ref{fig:sda}; Algorithm~\ref{alg:sda}, App.~\ref{app:training}).

\paragraph{Teacher signals.}
At the final passage position, the logit lens projects hidden states as $z_\ell(x)=W_U\,\mathrm{LN}(h_\ell(x))$ for $1\leq\ell\leq L$. Here $h_\ell$ is the residual state after layer $\ell$ for $\ell<L$; $h_L$ is the final normalized state returned by the model. The readout takes the peak reciprocal rank:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathrm{rank}_\ell(t)&=1+|\{v:z_{\ell,v}>z_{\ell,t}\}|,\\
R(c;x)&=\max_{\substack{1\leq\ell\leq L\\t\in F(c)}}\frac{1}{\mathrm{rank}_\ell(t)}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:wrr}
\end{equation}
$F(c)$ contains distinct first tokens of bare and leading-space forms of $c$, in original and capitalized case. One forward pass serves all candidates. Output likelihood is $\mathrm{LL}(c;x)=\log p(c\mid x)/|c|$, where $|c|$ is token length. Within-episode ranks place the signals on a common scale.

\paragraph{Labels and distillation.}
Let $P_R$ and $P_{\mathrm{LL}}$ be within-episode mid-rank percentiles, with ties assigned their mean rank. The teacher averages them:
\begin{equation}
T(c;x)=\tfrac12\bigl(P_R(c;x)+P_{\mathrm{LL}}(c;x)\bigr).
\label{eq:teacher}
\end{equation}
The top two candidates receive $\tilde y=1$ (\emph{yes}), the rest $\tilde y=0$ (\emph{no}); candidate order breaks ties.

With base weights frozen, rank-16 LoRA \citep{hu2021lora} on every attention and feed-forward projection minimizes target-token cross-entropy through the importance prompt:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal L(\theta)={}&-\frac{1}{|\mathcal D|}\sum_{(x,c)\in\mathcal D}\\
&\quad\cdot\log p_\theta\!\left(\tau(\tilde y(c;x))\mid u(c,x)\right).
\end{split}
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
$\mathcal D$ contains passage--candidate pairs; $\tau$ maps labels to target tokens. Probabilities are vocabulary-normalized; prompt tokens are masked. Training runs two epochs. We retain the earliest checkpoint with highest validation within-episode AUC; gold utility labels serve only validation (App.~\ref{app:training}). Results average three training runs.

\paragraph{Why the student can exceed its teacher.}
Condition B tests discrimination the judgment already possesses. Training retains the importance prompt while teacher labels counter its preference for passage words. The student thus starts with useful discrimination beyond its supervision, the premise of weak-to-strong generalization \citep{burns2023weak}; \S\ref{sec:learns} compares student and teacher.

\paragraph{Serving.}
SDA ranks by $s_\theta$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:score}) and stores the top $k$, using one yes/no forward pass per candidate with passage prefix caching \citep{kwon2023pagedattention}; the reader answers with the adapter off.

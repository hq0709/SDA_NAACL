\section{Diagnosing the Statedness Bias}\label{sec:diagnosis}

We test statedness preference (\S\ref{sec:bias}), rewording and statedness rules (\S\ref{sec:tradeoff}), and recognition of implied concepts (\S\ref{sec:knowledge}).

\subsection{Statedness Bias Dominates Importance Judgments}\label{sec:bias}\label{sec:gap}

Table~\ref{tab:grid} compares four-condition win rates for the importance report, the 1--10 importance rating, and SDA across six models.
\input{tables/tab_grid}

\myparagraph{Competence collapses against passage words.}
In condition B, the importance report ranks implied useful concepts above unstated distractors in 92.4\% of comparisons; against stated distractors in A, it wins 38.3\%. All six models exhibit the strong form (31.9--45.0\%). Condition D reaches 99.2\% when statedness favors the useful concept.

\myparagraph{Passage words look equally important.}
When both candidates are stated, the importance report wins 50.6\% (37.4--64.3\% across models). Once named, the useful concept joins the favored passage words, among which the report does not reliably distinguish usefulness.

\myparagraph{Misses fill memory with passage words.}
Across $k=1,2,3$, when the importance report misses an implied useful concept, 95.0--99.2\% of stored concepts are passage words.

\myparagraph{Scale does not remove the bias.}
The importance report's condition A rises from 34.9\% to 36.5\% to 38.7\% across Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B, and Qwen3-32B, respectively, with the strong form throughout.

\myparagraph{Other importance judgments share the preference.}
The 1--10 importance rating wins 58.2\% in A versus 91.0\% in B, reducing the failure without equalizing conditions. On Qwen3-8B, statedness correlations are $+0.90$ for single-pass ConsistencyGate, $+0.85$ for sampled ConsistencyGate, and $+0.75$ for A-MAC utility; implied retention at $k=2$ is 2.9\%, 5.2\%, and 25.4\%, respectively. On Qwen3-8B, all published admission criteria, including prediction error, have lower answer accuracy than the importance report at every budget.

\myparagraph{The original pool exposes the same preference.}
On the original pool, the importance report's pooled useful-concept AUC is 0.322--0.427, below chance in every model. It favors passage words although the useful concept is usually the sole unstated candidate.

\subsection{Statedness-Cued Wording and Statedness Rules Trade One Failure for Another}\label{sec:tradeoff}

Table~\ref{tab:split} separates retention and answer accuracy by useful-concept statedness at $k=2$: statedness still determines admission under the importance report and statedness rules.
\input{tables/tab_split}

\myparagraph{Statedness-cued wording reverses the preference.}
On Qwen3-8B, statedness-cued wording raises implied retention from 31.2\% to 49.7\% and lowers stated retention from 47.4\% to 30.5\%. Pooled useful-concept AUC rises from 0.322 to 0.527 on the original pool, falls from 0.515 to 0.410 on the original pool with the useful concept named, and falls from 0.710 to 0.621 on the 88 Explicit episodes, whose passages state the useful concept. Answer accuracy is 42.7\%, against the importance report's 43.0\%. Implied gains alone do not establish admission by usefulness. Implied-content wording raises retention for stated (47.4\% to 55.3\%) and implied episodes (31.2\% to 48.6\%), with accuracy near the importance report's (44.9\% versus 43.0\%).

\myparagraph{Unstated-first selection discards stated useful concepts.}
At $k=2$, unstated-first with report ordering yields six-model mean retention of 92.6\% on implied episodes and 0.0\% on stated episodes. Implied answer accuracy rises from the importance report's 31.5\% to 34.9\%; stated accuracy falls from 35.2\% to 29.9\%. Qwen3-8B overall accuracy falls from 43.0\% to 39.1\%.

\subsection{The Model Knows What Its Judgment Does Not Say}\label{sec:knowledge}

\myparagraph{Knowing without admitting.}
Among unstated candidates in the original pool, the importance report's pooled useful-concept AUC is 0.880--0.897. Together with condition B, this shows the model recognizes implied useful concepts; the failure lies in how its judgment ranks them against passage words.

\myparagraph{Internal signals show a weaker statedness preference.}
The logit-lens readout takes peak reciprocal ranks of candidate first tokens across layers; output likelihood uses length-normalized log probability under the passage. On the original pool, statedness AUCs are 0.396--0.477 and 0.353--0.401, against the importance report's 0.582--0.688: neither internal signal favors stated candidates. On the statedness-balanced pool, the readout's statedness AUC is 0.562--0.604, against 0.909--0.945 for the importance report.

\myparagraph{The logit-lens readout reads the passage.}
Replacing the passage lowers the readout's original-pool useful-concept AUC in all six models: Qwen3-8B falls from 0.543 to 0.433, while output likelihood changes from 0.613 to 0.604. The readout captures passage-dependent knowledge; output likelihood mostly reflects candidate priors.

\myparagraph{Useful concepts peak before the output.}
On the original pool, layers below the output yield useful-concept AUCs of 0.537--0.621, exceeding the next-token distribution's 0.503--0.591 in all six models. The median first peak lies one to four layers below the output (App.~\ref{app:peaklayer}). Section~\ref{sec:method} uses this knowledge to train admission.

\section{Background and Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Memory Admission}\label{sec:problem}

Memory admission selects the information available to a model after a passage is removed. Given passage $x$ and ordered candidates $C=(c_1,\ldots,c_m)$, memory admission stores $K\subseteq C$, $|K|\leq k$, before future question $q$ and answer $a$ are known; the frozen reader answers $q$ from $K$ and its own knowledge. All policies receive the same candidates and budget and use the same frozen reader, so comparisons isolate the admission decision. Importance judgments make this decision by scoring candidates and storing the top $k$.

The importance report turns a model's judgment of future usefulness into an admission score. The importance prompt $u(c,x)$ asks, based only on the passage, whether $c$ is important for possible future questions, requesting a single \emph{yes} or \emph{no} (App.~\ref{app:prompts}). Let $Y$ and $N$ contain distinct first tokens of the bare and leading-space forms of \emph{yes} and \emph{no}, in lowercase, initial-capital and uppercase forms:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
s_\theta(c;x)={}&\log\!\sum_{t\in Y}p_\theta(t\mid u(c,x))\\
&-\log\!\sum_{t\in N}p_\theta(t\mid u(c,x)),\\
V_\theta(c;x)={}&\sigma\!\left(s_\theta(c;x)\right).
\end{split}
\label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
Here, $p_\theta$ is the model's next-token distribution under the prompt. The score compares the total probability assigned to \emph{yes} with that assigned to \emph{no}; applying the sigmoid gives the \emph{yes} share of their combined probability. This transformation preserves the ordering, so higher values give a candidate priority under the memory budget. The untouched model's $V_\theta$ is the importance report. The 1--10 importance rating and published admission criteria also score candidates. To test whether passage words displace useful concepts under each, we distinguish statedness from usefulness.

\subsection{Stated and Implied Concepts}\label{sec:statedness}

Define statedness $S(c,x)=1$ when a case-insensitive stem match finds candidate $c$ in passage $x$, and $S(c,x)=0$ otherwise. Stated candidates are passage words. Each episode's useful concept $b$ links the passage to the future answer; other candidates are distractors. An unstated $b$ is \emph{implied}. In Figure~\ref{fig:episode}, \texttt{mesopotamia} is implied and useful, whereas \texttt{silt} is a stated distractor. Naming \texttt{mesopotamia} changes statedness without changing usefulness.

We measure the effect of distractor statedness through within-episode comparisons with the useful concept. For episode $i$, let $D_i^d$ contain distractors with statedness $d$, and $\mathcal E_{r,d}$ contain episodes with $S(b_i,x_i)=r$ and $D_i^d\neq\varnothing$. Write $s_i(c)=s(c;x_i)$. The win rate averages over these episodes and their distractors:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathrm{WR}_{r,d}(s)={}&\frac{1}{|\mathcal E_{r,d}|}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal E_{r,d}}\frac{1}{|D_i^d|}\\
&\quad\cdot\sum_{c\in D_i^d}\phi(s_i(b_i),s_i(c)),\\
\phi(u,v)={}&\mathbf{1}\{u>v\}+\tfrac12\mathbf{1}\{u=v\}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:winrate}
\end{equation}
The function $\phi$ counts a win when the useful concept scores higher and gives ties half credit. The inner average measures its win rate against distractors of the specified statedness, and the outer average gives each eligible episode equal weight.

A judgment exhibits the \textbf{Statedness Bias} when $\mathrm{WR}_{0,1}<\mathrm{WR}_{0,0}$: implied useful concepts win less often against stated than against unstated distractors. The \textbf{strong form}, $\mathrm{WR}_{0,1}<1/2$, means the judgment prefers a stated distractor to the useful concept. An unbiased judgment gives the same win rate whatever the distractor's statedness, holding useful-concept statedness fixed. We also measure the preference for stated candidates with statedness AUC, the probability that a stated candidate outranks an unstated one, counting ties half.

\subsection{Related Work}\label{sec:relwork}

\paragraph{Memory systems and admission.}
Memory research studies both the organization of persistent information and the criteria used to retain it. Generative Agents uses importance alongside recency and relevance \citep{park2023generative}, while MemoryBank, MemGPT, Mem0, and A-MEM organize persistent memory \citep{zhong2023memorybank,packer2023memgpt,mem0,amem2025}. LoCoMo and LongMemEval evaluate conversational memory \citep{maharana2024locomo,wu2025longmemeval}, while MemCon and AgeMem learn memory operations \citep{memcon2026,agemem2026}; \citet{hu2025memorysurvey} surveys these systems. At admission, approaches use contextual support \citep{consistencygate2026,amac2026}, evidence retention \citep{valuemodel2026}, memory interventions for known requests \citep{cmi2026}, and prediction error \citep{nemori2026}. These criteria assign scores to candidate memories; our diagnosis asks whether those scores preserve useful knowledge when the passage leaves it unstated.

\paragraph{Verbal reports and internal knowledge.}
An importance judgment is a verbal report, and prior work shows that such reports do not exhaust the information in a model's internal states. Internal states predict correctness beyond expressed confidence \citep{kadavath2022know,azaria2023internal,orgad2024llmsknow}. Verbal reports also omit reasoning influences and salience \citep{turpin2023cot,trienes2025salience}, while introspection training teaches models to predict their behavior \citep{binder2024introspection}. We apply this comparison to importance judgments by asking whether internal signals distinguish useful implied concepts that the report deprioritizes.

\paragraph{Readouts of internal states.}
Readouts provide a way to examine this difference between an importance report and internal knowledge. The logit lens projects hidden states into token predictions \citep{nostalgebraist2020logitlens}; related approaches include tuned lenses, Future Lens, Patchscopes \citep{belrose2023tuned,pal2023futurelens,ghandeharioun2024patchscopes}, and Jacobian-based lenses for verbalizable concepts \citep{anthropic2026workspace}. We use the training-free logit lens as an SDA teacher signal (\S\ref{sec:sda}) and to test what the model knows (\S\ref{sec:knowledge}).

\paragraph{Distillation and weak supervision.}
Distillation transfers teacher predictions to a student \citep{hinton2015distilling,furlanello2018bornagain}. In memory research, TMEM distills experience into low-rank weights \citep{tmem2026}, and Agent Memory Distillation transfers larger-agent trajectories into smaller-agent memory \citep{amd2026}. Strong students trained on weaker labels can also exceed their supervisors \citep{burns2023weak}. These lines of work leave a gap at the admission decision: none of these works separates statedness from usefulness in admission or trains importance judgments on the model's own signals.

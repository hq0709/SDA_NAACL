\subsection{Setup}\label{sec:setup}\label{sec:construction}\label{sec:benchmarks}\label{sec:breadth}\label{sec:models}

\paragraph{Episodes and pools.}
Test episodes follow the Decoupled construction: recover the useful concept, then combine it with general knowledge. GPT-4.1 writes a passage about a named person together with the useful concept, question and answer. The answer is excluded from passage and candidates; the question connects to the passage only through the person's name. The generator's gold utility labels mark the useful concept positive and other candidates negative; rule-based and blind alias filters check episodes (App.~\ref{app:benchmarks}).

A candidate is stated if a case-insensitive stem match finds it in the passage, and unstated otherwise. Statedness AUC replaces utility labels with statedness labels.

The original pool contains the generator's candidates for 363 test-set episodes: 1,607 candidates, 4.43 per episode. In 95\% of episodes, the useful concept is the sole unstated candidate, so admitting unstated candidates first exploits this composition.

The statedness-balanced pool is the main evaluation pool. In fixed test-set order, odd positions keep the passage; even positions append ``The passage also mentions \emph{concept}.'', naming the useful concept. Episodes retain original candidates and add the first four (odd positions) or three (even positions) unstated distractors from the pool with unstated distractors (App.~\ref{app:pools}). The pool has 190 stated-concept and 173 unstated-concept episodes, averaging 7.93 candidates; useful concepts constitute 13.3\% of stated and 12.0\% of unstated candidates. A statedness-only rule ranks near chance: pooled AUC 0.515, within-episode AUC 0.516. Every pool preserves questions, answers and utility labels; added candidates are negative.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Within-episode AUC averages correct useful-concept comparisons, giving ties half credit. For useful concept $b_i$, score $s$ and $n$ episodes:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
A_{\mathrm{episode}}&=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\sum_{c\in C_i\setminus\{b_i\}}\phi(s(b_i),s(c))}{|C_i|-1},\\
\phi(u,v)&=\mathbf{1}\{u>v\}+\tfrac12\mathbf{1}\{u=v\}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:episodeauc}
\end{equation}
Pooled AUC compares candidates across episodes. Useful-concept retention is $B_k=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}\{b_i\in K_i\}$. Answer accuracy is the fraction of questions answered correctly by a fixed frozen reader using greedy decoding, with Qwen3 thinking disabled and a blind semantic judge. String-match agreement is 85.1\%; SDA exceeds the importance report under both graders (App.~\ref{app:judge}). Gold-concept selection ranks the useful concept first as a reference row; closed-book (question only) and full-context (whole passage) readers provide references.

\paragraph{Models and baselines.}
SDA covers six models: Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B, Qwen3-32B, Qwen2.5-7B, OLMo-2-7B and Mistral-7B \citep{qwen2025,olmo2,jiang2023mistral,qwen3}. Training and validation use 175 and 45 episodes from Explicit and implied-answer constructions (App.~\ref{app:training}). Development sets select configurations; the 363-episode test set and 316-episode held-out set serve only evaluation, with budgets $k=1,2,3$.

Baselines comprise the untrained model's importance report and 1--10 importance rating, rewordings of the importance prompt, ConsistencyGate, A-MAC, Nemori and statedness rules admitting unstated candidates first (App.~\ref{app:baselines}). Trained results average three training runs. Serving and useful-concept replacement use one reference run. Bold table entries identify SDA.

\subsection{Importance Judgments Favor Stated Candidates}\label{sec:gap}

The importance report favors stated candidates when stated and unstated candidates compete. Among unstated candidates, its pooled AUC is 0.880--0.897 across six models, against 0.592--0.697 for the workspace readout (Table~\ref{tab:withinunstated}). Importance report AUC falls to 0.658--0.736 on the statedness-balanced pool and below chance, 0.322--0.427, on the original pool, where its statedness AUC is 0.582--0.688. On the statedness-balanced pool, correlations with statedness are $+0.90$ for single-pass ConsistencyGate, $+0.85$ for sampled ConsistencyGate and $+0.75$ for A-MAC utility.

The workspace readout ranks useful concepts higher than the importance report on the statedness-balanced pool (pooled AUC 0.719--0.812) and above chance on the original pool (six-model mean 0.563). The stated-bridge control, which appends ``The passage also mentions \emph{concept}.'' naming the useful concept, raises mean workspace readout AUC to 0.956; importance report AUC rises from 0.373 to 0.504 (Figure~\ref{fig:hero}a, Table~\ref{tab:statedcontrol}). Naming a distractor lowers both on Qwen3-8B (App.~\ref{app:naming}).

We observe that SDA improves answer accuracy over the reworded importance prompts, namely the implied-content wording and the statedness-cued wording (Table~\ref{tab:balancedbaselines}, App.~\ref{app:wording}).

The model's intermediate layers encode information about unstated useful concepts that its importance report does not express; SDA turns this information into admission decisions (\S\ref{sec:method}).

\subsection{Admission Results}\label{sec:recall}

SDA exceeds each model's importance report and 1--10 importance rating in all 18 model--budget comparisons on the statedness-balanced pool (Figure~\ref{fig:results}). Qwen3-8B answer accuracy at $k=2$ rises from 43.0\% to 53.7\%. The 1--10 importance rating is its strongest untrained policy on this pool; SDA exceeds it by 5.2 points at $k=2$.

Qwen3-8B SDA exceeds every baseline at every budget, reaching 40.5\%, 53.7\% and 51.8\% at $k=1,2,3$ (Table~\ref{tab:balancedbaselines}). \subsection{Ablations}\label{sec:ablations}\label{sec:scale}

Teacher-signal ablations train the same adapter after removing the workspace readout or output likelihood, fixing training episodes and step budget. On the statedness-balanced pool, removing the workspace readout lowers unstated useful-concept retention by 6.6, 4.5 and 2.5 points at $k=1,2,3$, in all five models: Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B, Qwen3-32B, Qwen2.5-7B and OLMo-2-7B. Removing output likelihood changes it by at most 0.8 points and lowers stated useful-concept retention by 2.2, 1.9 and 1.8 points at $k=1,2,3$ (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}a,b; Table~\ref{tab:teacherretention}). Across all episodes, removing output likelihood lowers answer accuracy by 1.6 and 1.7 points at $k=1,2$; removing the workspace readout lowers it by 0.7, 0.6 and 0.3 at $k=1,2,3$ (Table~\ref{tab:teacherretention}; per-model accuracy in Table~\ref{tab:teachermodels}).

Pooling unstated-concept episodes for SDA and students without the workspace readout, the frozen reader answers 47.9\% correctly with the useful concept among stored concepts and 29.8\% without it (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}d). Replacing it with SDA's next-ranked candidate on the 249 episodes where SDA retains it lowers Qwen3-8B answer accuracy from 54.6\% to 37.8\%, a paired decrease of 16.9 points, losing 59 answers and gaining 17.

Both ablations lower mean answer accuracy at every budget on the paired-passage set. Paired test-set episodes share candidates in different orders; the partner's useful concept is negative, so only the passage identifies usefulness (App.~\ref{app:pools}). Across 181 pairs and 1,810 observations per budget, removing the workspace readout lowers useful-concept retention by 7.3 points and answer accuracy by 1.9 at $k=1$; removing output likelihood lowers answer accuracy by 1.3. Removing the workspace readout lowers useful-concept retention at every budget (Table~\ref{tab:crosspairpooled}).

Passage replacement reveals dependence on the passage by substituting another episode's passage with candidates, labels and question fixed. On the original pool at $k=1$, Qwen3-8B SDA useful-concept retention falls by 20.9 points; the student without output likelihood loses 26.5 points, and the student without the workspace readout loses 2.9 points (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}c).

For Qwen3-8B on the statedness-balanced pool at $k=2$, the combined teacher used directly reaches 44.9\% answer accuracy, 8.8 points below SDA; output likelihood used directly is 11.8 points below (Table~\ref{tab:balanceddirect}, Table~\ref{tab:balancedpaired}). SDA trained through implied-content wording exceeds that wording at every budget (Table~\ref{tab:wordingdistill}). At every tested Qwen3 scale, SDA raises pooled AUC on the development sets while the adapter-enabled model's workspace readout AUC changes by at most 0.0046 (Table~\ref{tab:sdascale}).

\subsection{Deployment}\label{sec:deployment}

SDA serves 242 candidates per second at 4.1\,ms per candidate through vLLM on one A100-80GB, against 290 at 3.4\,ms for the importance report, using prefix caching over the original pool's 1,607 candidates after warm-up. Served Qwen3-8B answer accuracy is 50.1\%, within 0.3 points of the reference run's offline selections; top-2 stored concepts agree on 91.7\% of episodes (App.~\ref{app:timing}). Enabling the adapter on every token changes four capability scores by at most 0.3 points (App.~\ref{app:capability}).

\section{Results and Analysis}\label{sec:results}\label{sec:recall}\label{sec:diagnosis}

This section presents the diagnostic and SDA results. We ask four questions: whether importance judgments prefer stated candidates (\S\ref{sec:bias}), whether rewording and statedness rules remove the preference (\S\ref{sec:tradeoff}), whether the model knows the implied concept (\S\ref{sec:knowledge}), and whether SDA reduces the bias and what it learns (\S\ref{sec:correct}, \S\ref{sec:learns}).

\subsection{Statedness Bias Dominates Importance Judgments}\label{sec:bias}\label{sec:gap}

The importance report favors statedness strongly enough to rank implied useful concepts below stated distractors. Table~\ref{tab:grid} compares four-condition win rates for the importance report, the 1--10 importance rating, and SDA across six models. The contrasts show how usefulness and statedness each affect the ranking.
\input{tables/tab_grid}

\myparagraph{Competence collapses against passage words.}
The importance report distinguishes useful implied content from unstated distractors, yet fails when the distractors are passage words. In condition B, it ranks implied useful concepts above unstated distractors in 92.4\% of comparisons; against stated distractors in A, it wins 38.3\%. All six models exhibit the strong form (31.9--45.0\%). Condition D reaches 99.2\% when statedness favors the useful concept. Thus, the report recognizes usefulness among unstated alternatives, but statedness changes which candidate it favors. The comparison among stated candidates tests whether it distinguishes usefulness within this favored group.

\myparagraph{Passage words look equally important.}
The preference for passage words is accompanied by weak discrimination among them. When both candidates are stated, the importance report wins 50.6\% (37.4--64.3\% across models). Once named, the useful concept joins the favored passage words, among which the report does not reliably distinguish usefulness. This combination of preference across groups and weak discrimination within the stated group shapes what enters memory.

\myparagraph{Misses fill memory with passage words.}
Failed admission leaves memory dominated by stated content. Across $k=1,2,3$, when the importance report misses an implied useful concept, 95.0--99.2\% of stored concepts are passage words. The ranking preference therefore carries through to memory contents: passage words occupy the available slots while useful implied content is omitted.

\myparagraph{The bias persists across Qwen3 scales.}
Increasing model size improves the critical comparison without removing its below-chance ranking. The importance report's condition A rises from 34.9\% to 36.5\% to 38.7\% across Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B, and Qwen3-32B, respectively, with the strong form throughout. Scale changes the degree of failure within this family, while the preference for stated distractors persists. We next examine whether changing the importance judgment changes this pattern.

\myparagraph{Other importance judgments share the preference.}
Alternative judgments retain a statedness preference, although its strength differs. The 1--10 importance rating wins 58.2\% in A versus 91.0\% in B, avoiding the strong form without equalizing conditions. On Qwen3-8B, statedness correlations are $+0.90$ for single-pass ConsistencyGate, $+0.85$ for sampled ConsistencyGate, and $+0.75$ for A-MAC utility; implied retention at $k=2$ is 2.9\%, 5.2\%, and 25.4\%, respectively. On Qwen3-8B, all published admission criteria, including prediction error, have lower answer accuracy than the importance report at every budget. The preference therefore extends beyond the yes/no importance report, and changing criteria does not ensure better answers.

\myparagraph{The original pool exposes the same preference.}
The failure also appears without the statedness-balancing construction. On the original pool, the importance report's pooled useful-concept AUC is 0.322--0.427, below chance in every model. The preference observed in the controlled comparisons therefore extends to the original candidate pool.

Across candidate contrasts, model scales, and importance judgments, statedness shapes admission. The report recognizes implied useful concepts among unstated alternatives, yet ranks them below passage words and fills memory with those words when it misses. This pattern motivates interventions that explicitly direct attention toward unstated content.

\subsection{Statedness-Cued Wording and Statedness Rules Trade One Failure for Another}\label{sec:tradeoff}

Statedness-cued wording and unstated-first selection improve implied retention at the expense of stated useful concepts. Table~\ref{tab:split} separates retention and answer accuracy by useful-concept statedness at $k=2$: statedness still determines admission under the importance report and statedness rules. Separating the episode types shows whether gains for implied content come with losses elsewhere.
\input{tables/tab_split}

\myparagraph{Statedness-cued wording reverses the preference.}
The statedness cue shifts admission toward implied useful concepts while reducing admission of stated ones. On Qwen3-8B at $k=2$, statedness-cued wording raises implied retention from 31.2\% to 49.7\% and lowers stated retention from 47.4\% to 30.5\%. Pooled useful-concept AUC rises from 0.322 to 0.527 on the original pool, falls from 0.515 to 0.410 on the original pool with the useful concept named, and falls from 0.710 to 0.621 on the 88 Explicit episodes, whose passages state the useful concept. Answer accuracy is 42.7\%, against the importance report's 43.0\%. These opposing changes show that the wording reverses the preference. In contrast, implied-content wording raises retention for stated (47.4\% to 55.3\%) and implied episodes (31.2\% to 48.6\%), with accuracy near the importance report's (44.9\% versus 43.0\%). Wording thus changes which concepts are retained without ensuring a corresponding gain in answers. An unstated-first rule tests the consequence of enforcing the preference directly in selection.

\myparagraph{Unstated-first selection discards stated useful concepts.}
Giving unstated candidates priority produces a sharper trade-off between the episode types. At $k=2$, unstated-first with report ordering yields six-model mean retention of 92.6\% on implied episodes and 0.0\% on stated episodes. Implied answer accuracy rises from the importance report's 31.5\% to 34.9\%; stated accuracy falls from 35.2\% to 29.9\%. Qwen3-8B overall accuracy falls from 43.0\% to 39.1\%. High implied retention therefore comes with the loss of stated useful concepts, and the implied gains do not offset the stated losses in overall answer accuracy on Qwen3-8B.

The statedness cue and the unstated-first rule trade missed implied concepts for missed stated concepts. Implied-content wording improves retention in both groups, but answer accuracy remains near the report's. These results shift the question from which statedness group to favor to whether the model has useful information that its importance judgment fails to use.

\subsection{The Model Knows What Its Judgment Does Not Say}\label{sec:knowledge}

Recognition of implied useful concepts and their admission to memory are dissociated. Comparisons among unstated candidates establish recognition, while internal signals show how useful information depends on the passage and where it appears before the output.

\myparagraph{Knowing without admitting.}
The importance report recognizes implied useful concepts when passage words are excluded from the comparison. Among the unstated candidates of the pool with unstated distractors, its pooled useful-concept AUC is 0.880--0.897 (App.~\ref{app:withinprovenance}). Together with condition B, this shows that the failure lies in how the judgment ranks recognized implied concepts against passage words. We therefore examine whether internal signals express the same statedness preference as the report.

\myparagraph{Internal signals show a weaker statedness preference.}
Internal signals favor passage words less than the importance report does. The logit-lens readout takes peak reciprocal ranks of candidate first tokens across layers; output likelihood uses length-normalized log probability under the passage. On the original pool, statedness AUCs are 0.396--0.477 and 0.353--0.401, against the importance report's 0.582--0.688: neither internal signal favors stated candidates. On the statedness-balanced pool, the readout's statedness AUC is 0.562--0.604, against 0.909--0.945 for the importance report. The readout therefore retains a weaker statedness preference on the balanced pool. To understand what its ranking captures, we next test its dependence on the passage.

\myparagraph{The logit-lens readout reads the passage.}
The readout's useful-concept ranking depends on passage information. Replacing the passage lowers its original-pool useful-concept AUC in all six models: Qwen3-8B falls from 0.543 to 0.433, while output likelihood changes from 0.613 to 0.604. On Qwen3-8B, output likelihood mostly reflects candidate priors. This contrast connects the readout to the passage and motivates examining where its useful signal appears across layers.

\myparagraph{Useful concepts peak before the output.}
Layers below the output distinguish useful concepts more accurately than the final next-token distribution. On the original pool, these layers yield useful-concept AUCs of 0.537--0.621, exceeding the next-token distribution's 0.503--0.591 in all six models. The median first peak lies one to four layers below the output (App.~\ref{app:peaklayer}). Useful-concept information is therefore available before the final prediction, where its discrimination is weaker.

Together, these results show that the importance judgment fails to make full use of available information. The report recognizes implied useful concepts among unstated alternatives, and the readout captures passage-sensitive information before the output, yet admission favors passage words. The dissociation implies that recognizing a useful concept does not ensure that the model ranks it for storage: the Statedness Bias arises in how available information is translated into an admission judgment. SDA (\S\ref{sec:sda}) uses this knowledge to train admission; the following subsections test its effect on the bias and what it learns.

\subsection{Useful Concepts Outrank Stated Distractors}\label{sec:correct}

\myparagraph{Win rates.}
SDA removes the strong form of the Statedness Bias in every model while leaving a gap between conditions A and B. On the statedness-balanced pool, condition A's mean win rate rises from 38.3\% to 67.5\%, with model-level rates of 58.9--77.2\%. Condition C rises from 50.6\% to 79.5\%, showing improved discrimination among passage words, while conditions B and D remain high at 92.5\% and 98.9\% (Table~\ref{tab:grid}). The gap between conditions A and B narrows from 54.1 to 25.0 points. Thus, implied useful concepts now outrank stated distractors more often than they lose, although stated distractors still disadvantage them relative to unstated distractors.

\myparagraph{Retention and accuracy.}
The ranking gains translate into higher useful-concept retention and answer accuracy for both episode groups. On the statedness-balanced pool at $k=2$, six-model mean retention rises from 45.3\% to 80.3\% on stated episodes and 33.4\% to 63.8\% on implied episodes (Table~\ref{tab:split}). In every model, SDA exceeds the importance report, the 1--10 importance rating, unstated-first with report ordering, unstated-first, and the reversed importance report in both groups' answer accuracy. On Qwen3-8B at the same budget, overall accuracy reaches 53.7\% versus 43.0\%, 48.5\%, and 39.1\% for the importance report, rating, and unstated-first with report ordering. The published criteria reach 39.7\% answer accuracy for A-MAC utility, 35.0\% for both ConsistencyGate forms, and 25.9\% for prediction error, against SDA's 53.7\% (Table~\ref{tab:split}). These results connect improved admission to better answers while retaining useful concepts more often in both groups.

\myparagraph{Budgets and pools.}
The accuracy gains extend across memory budgets and candidate pools. SDA exceeds the importance report and rating in all 18 model-budget comparisons on the statedness-balanced pool (App.~\ref{app:balancedresults}). Against the importance report, it wins all 18 on the original pool (mean gain: 9.6 points at $k=2$) and 17 of 18 on the naming-balanced pool; the remaining comparison trails by 0.1 points. On the pool with unstated distractors, SDA exceeds the importance report for every model at $k=2$ (Table~\ref{tab:stress}). Qwen3-8B gains 11.0 points on the enlarged pool at $k=2$ and exceeds the importance report on every development set and the held-out set (App.~\ref{app:poolresults}; App.~\ref{app:heldout}). The advantage therefore persists across changes in the budget, distractor supply, and evaluation split.

\subsection{What SDA Learns}\label{sec:learns}\label{sec:ablations}

\myparagraph{Student and teacher.}
On Qwen3-8B, SDA builds on the importance report's ranking and outperforms direct use of the teacher. On the statedness-balanced pool, SDA's within-episode rank correlation is 0.76 with the importance report and 0.22 with the teacher; direct teacher use yields 44.9\% answer accuracy at $k=2$, 8.8 points below SDA. Where SDA's and the teacher's top candidates differ, SDA selects the useful concept in 93 episodes versus the teacher's 85 on the statedness-balanced pool, and 119 versus 37 on the original pool. Together, these results support the method's account: the student stays close to the report's ranking while teacher supervision reduces its preference for passage words.

\myparagraph{Teacher ablations.}
The teacher signals contribute to different parts of this improvement: the readout supports implied retention, and output likelihood supports stated retention. On the statedness-balanced pool, removing the logit-lens readout lowers implied retention in all five ablated models (all except Mistral-7B) at every budget: mean losses are 6.6, 4.5, and 2.5 points at $k=1,2,3$. Removing output likelihood lowers stated retention by 2.2, 1.9, and 1.8 points, respectively. Passage replacement on Qwen3-8B's original pool at $k=1$ lowers SDA retention by 20.9 points versus 2.9 for the student trained without the readout. This larger drop shows that the readout makes admission more dependent on the passage.

\myparagraph{Knowledge and capabilities.}
The change in admission is accompanied by stable readout and capability scores. At all three Qwen3 scales, adapter-enabled readout AUC changes by at most 0.0046; enabling the adapter on every token changes four capability scores by at most 0.3 points (App.~\ref{app:capability}). These results separate improved admission from changes in the tested knowledge and capability measures.

\myparagraph{Deployment.}\label{sec:deployment}

SDA's served accuracy remains close to its offline accuracy. With prefix caching, vLLM running Qwen3-8B on one A100-80GB serves 242 candidates per second (4.1\,ms each), versus the importance report's 290 (3.4\,ms), over the original pool's 1,607 candidates. Served answer accuracy on the original pool is 50.1\%, within 0.3 points of offline, with 91.7\% agreement in the top two stored concepts (App.~\ref{app:timing}). The admission improvement therefore carries into this serving configuration, with lower throughput than the importance report.

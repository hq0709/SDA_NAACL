\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Statedness and Importance Judgments}
\label{sec:gap}

The importance report ranks useful concepts well among unstated
candidates but loses that ordering when stated and unstated candidates
compete. Among unstated candidates, its pooled AUC is 0.880--0.897
across six models, compared with 0.592--0.697 for the workspace
readout (Table~\ref{tab:withinunstated}). Report AUC falls to
0.658--0.736 on the statedness-balanced pool and below chance to
0.322--0.427 on the original pool, where statedness AUC is
0.582--0.688.

The workspace readout ranks useful concepts higher than the importance
report on the statedness-balanced pool, with pooled AUC 0.719--0.812.
Statedness alone ranks candidates near chance on this pool
(\S\ref{sec:construction}).

On the original pool, the six-model mean workspace
AUC is above chance at 0.563. Naming the useful concept raises it to
0.956 while report AUC rises from 0.373 to 0.504
(Table~\ref{tab:statedcontrol}). Naming a distractor lowers both on
Qwen3-8B (App.~\ref{app:naming}). Statedness-cued wording still responds
to naming, in the opposite direction: Qwen3-8B AUC falls from 0.537
when the useful concept is unstated to 0.417 when named; it is 0.621
on Explicit
(App.~\ref{app:wording}).

In 95\% of original-pool episodes, the useful concept is the sole
unstated candidate. At $k=2$, unstated-first and unstated-first with
report ordering both reach 53.4\% answer accuracy; the reversed
importance report reaches 46.6\%. Balancing statedness lowers
answer accuracy for all three policies because their preference for
unstated candidates now also selects the added unstated distractors.
All three then rank below the importance report at
$k=2,3$; unstated-first with report ordering exceeds it at $k=1$
(Tables~\ref{tab:balancedbaselines}, \ref{tab:baselines}
and~\ref{tab:paired}).

On the statedness-balanced pool, SDA exceeds unstated-first with
report ordering by $14.6$ points at $k=2$. On the original pool, where
the useful concept is the sole unstated candidate in 95\% of episodes,
this rule leads SDA by $9.5$ points at $k=1$ and $3.4$ points at $k=2$
(Tables~\ref{tab:paired} and~\ref{tab:balancedpaired}).

Qwen3-8B SDA reaches 40.5\%, 53.7\% and 51.8\% answer accuracy at
$k=1,2,3$ on the statedness-balanced pool
(Table~\ref{tab:balancedbaselines}). At $k=2$, it exceeds the 1--10

Published admission criteria also fall below the untrained importance
report on the statedness-balanced pool. We implemented ConsistencyGate
\citep{consistencygate2026}, A-MAC utility \citep{amac2026}, and Nemori's
prediction-error criterion \citep{nemori2026} from their papers.
Each admits top-$k$ candidates under the same budget protocol
(Table~\ref{tab:balancedbaselines}, Figure~\ref{fig:results}a).
For Qwen3-8B at $k=2$, SDA exceeds each ConsistencyGate form by
$18.7$ points, A-MAC utility by $14.1$ and prediction error by $27.8$.
The smallest margin is $6.9$ points over A-MAC utility at $k=3$ (Table~\ref{tab:balancedpaired}).

ConsistencyGate and A-MAC utility track whether the passage states a
candidate. On the statedness-balanced pool, their correlations with
statedness are $+0.90$ for single-pass ConsistencyGate, $+0.85$ for
sampled ConsistencyGate, and $+0.75$ for A-MAC utility. On the standard
pool, ConsistencyGate's 0.7 threshold admits 26.7\% of useful concepts
against 70.8\% of all candidates. Its within-episode AUC is 0.133 for
single-pass scoring and 0.190 for sampled scoring.

A single statedness offset for stated candidates, fitted on development
episodes, places every unstated candidate ahead of every stated
candidate, turning the report into unstated-first with report ordering.
It raises development AUC from 0.304 to 0.978 while lowering
statedness-balanced AUC from 0.683 to 0.575. Accuracy matches this
rule at $k=2,3$; at $k=1$, the offset and rule reach 36.6\% and 36.9\%,
respectively. Unlike this offset, SDA's ranking depends on the passage
(\S\ref{sec:ablations}, Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}c).

SDA improves retention of unstated useful concepts and also retains
stated useful concepts. For Qwen3-8B at $k=2$, the
importance report retains the useful concept in 47.4\% of
stated-concept episodes and 31.2\% of unstated-concept episodes.
The workspace readout reaches 90.0\% and 35.8\%; SDA reaches 77.5\%
and 63.4\%.

In the 173 unstated-concept episodes, SDA reaches 37.4\%, 50.1\% and 46.6\% answer accuracy at
$k=1,2,3$, compared with 24.9\%, 40.5\% and 37.0\% for
the importance report. The gains are $12.5$, $9.6$ and $9.6$ points,
respectively. SDA also exceeds the 1--10 importance rating by $5.6$
points at $k=2$.

On the original pool, SDA exceeds each model's importance report in
all 18 model--budget comparisons, with a mean gain of 9.6 points at
$k=2$ (Tables~\ref{tab:sdabudget} and~\ref{tab:paired}), and improves
Qwen3-8B within-episode ranking and retention
(Table~\ref{tab:episodemetrics}).

Retaining the useful concept matters for answer accuracy. On the 249 episodes
where SDA retains it, we replace that concept with SDA's next-ranked
candidate. The frozen reader's answer accuracy falls from 54.6\% to
37.8\%, a paired decrease of 16.9 points; the replacement loses 59
answers and gains 17.

\paragraph{Reader references and candidate extraction.}
The closed-book and full-context readers reach 22.9\% and 63.9\%
answer accuracy on Qwen3-8B, bracketing SDA's 50.0\% on the original
pool. A context-only candidate extractor proposes the useful concept
in only 1.4\% of episodes, and SDA reaches 34.0\% at $k=2$ on its
candidates
(Table~\ref{tab:readerproposal}; extraction prompt in
App.~\ref{app:prompts}).

\subsection{Robustness Across Candidate Pools}
\label{sec:budgets}

SDA's answer accuracy gains extend to the naming-balanced pool, where
naming carries no utility information, to the pool with unstated
distractors, and to the enlarged pool.
On the naming-balanced pool, SDA exceeds each model's importance
report in 17 of 18 model--budget comparisons; the remaining comparison
differs by 0.1 points (Table~\ref{tab:namingrecall}). SDA exceeds the
1--10 importance rating in all twelve comparisons at $k=1,2$; at $k=3$,
it exceeds the rating for two of six models and trails it by at most
2.6 points. Qwen3-8B also improves within-episode ranking and
useful-concept retention
(Table~\ref{tab:namingbalanced}).

On the pool with unstated distractors, SDA exceeds each model's
importance report in every model--budget comparison
(Table~\ref{tab:stress}). For Qwen3-8B, SDA also exceeds output
likelihood used directly at every budget on this pool and the
enlarged pool (Tables~\ref{tab:baselines} and~\ref{tab:bigpool}).

At $k=2$ on the pool with unstated distractors, SDA exceeds the
importance report by 9.6 accuracy points, the 1--10 importance rating
by 4.1, and unstated-first with report ordering by 5.8
(Table~\ref{tab:paired}). On the enlarged pool, the corresponding
gains are 11.0, 4.7, and 6.6 points (Table~\ref{tab:paired}).

At $k=2,3$, SDA leads every measured baseline on both pools
(App.~\ref{app:poolresults}). At $k=1$, unstated-first with report
ordering leads SDA by $8.4$ points on the pool with unstated
distractors and by $5.5$ points on the enlarged pool
(Table~\ref{tab:paired}).

\subsection{Ablations}
\label{sec:ablations}
\label{sec:scale}

\paragraph{Teacher-signal ablations.}
Removing either teacher signal lowers answer accuracy on the
paired-passage set. We remove either the workspace readout or output
likelihood from SDA's teacher and train the same adapter on the
resulting labels, keeping training episodes and the step budget fixed.

Each candidate set appears twice under two passages with corresponding
questions and different useful concepts. Both episodes include their
partner's useful concept, so only the passage identifies which concept
is useful. We score 181 pairs on Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B, Qwen3-32B,
Qwen2.5-7B, and OLMo-2-7B, yielding 1,810 observations per budget
(Table~\ref{tab:crosspairpooled}; per-model results in
Table~\ref{tab:crosspairmodels}).

At $k=1$, removing the workspace readout lowers answer
accuracy by $1.9$ points and retention by $7.3$ points; removing
output likelihood lowers answer accuracy by $1.3$ points, and
retention rises by $1.4$ points. Both ablations lower mean accuracy,
and removing the workspace readout also lowers retention, at every
budget (Table~\ref{tab:crosspairpooled}). Differences are SDA minus
the trained ablation, averaged over the three training runs and pooled
over models.

\input{tables/tab_crosspair_pooled}

Passage replacement measures how the student's selections depend on
the passage (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}c). We score each episode before
and after replacing its passage with another episode's passage,
keeping candidates, labels, and the question fixed. On 363
original-pool episodes at $k=1$, Qwen3-8B SDA retention falls by
$20.9$ points and the student trained without output likelihood loses
$26.5$ points. The student trained without the workspace readout loses
only $2.9$ points, so its selections barely depend on the passage
(Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}c).

On the statedness-balanced pool, removing the workspace readout
lowers useful-concept retention in unstated-concept episodes by $6.6$,
$4.5$ and $2.5$ points at $k=1,2,3$, in all five models at every
budget, whereas removing output likelihood changes it by at most
$0.8$ points. In stated-concept episodes, removing output likelihood
lowers retention by $2.2$, $1.9$ and $1.8$ points at $k=1,2,3$
(Table~\ref{tab:teacherretention}, Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}a,b).
The workspace readout's contribution thus concentrates on unstated useful
concepts, while output likelihood supports useful concepts the passage
states.

Retention gains translate only partly into answer accuracy. On
unstated-concept episodes across SDA and the students trained without
the workspace readout, the frozen reader answers 47.9\% of questions
correctly when the useful concept is in memory and 29.8\% when it is not,
across five models, three training runs and three budgets
(Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}d). Across all episodes, removing output
likelihood lowers answer accuracy by $1.6$ and $1.7$ points at $k=1,2$;
removing the workspace readout lowers it by $0.7$, $0.6$ and $0.3$ points
at $k=1,2,3$ (Table~\ref{tab:teachermodelpaired}). This analysis combines
five models and 1,815 observations per budget, with per-model answer
accuracy in Table~\ref{tab:teachermodels}.

\paragraph{Distillation versus direct teacher scores.}
On the original pool at $k=2$, SDA reaches 50.0\% answer accuracy,
compared with 45.7\% for the combined teacher used directly and
40.2--40.5\% for students trained with a yes/no log-odds objective
instead of token prediction
(Tables~\ref{tab:sdaablation} and~\ref{tab:objective};
App.~\ref{app:objective}).
For Qwen3-8B on the original pool at $k=2$, useful-concept retention
is 69.1\% for SDA, 64.0\% for the student trained without the workspace
readout and 67.2\% for the student trained without output likelihood.

To separate training gains from prompt changes, we train and evaluate
Qwen3-8B SDA through the \emph{implied-content wording}, which permits
inferences without saying whether the passage states the useful
concept. This wording raises original-pool report AUC from 0.321 to
0.462 and improves answer accuracy at every budget
(Table~\ref{tab:wordingdistill}). With this wording, SDA reaches
37.1\%, 50.7\% and 51.0\% answer accuracy at $k=1,2,3$. It exceeds
the untrained implied-content wording at every budget
(Table~\ref{tab:wordingdistill}). SDA trained with the default
importance prompt reaches 37.6\%, 50.0\% and
52.2\% at $k=1,2,3$
(Tables~\ref{tab:baselines} and~\ref{tab:paired}).

\subsection{Deployment}
\label{sec:deployment}

Served through vLLM on one A100-80GB, SDA scores 242 candidates per
second (Table~\ref{tab:sdadeploy}). The served Qwen3-8B adapter reaches
50.1\% answer accuracy on the original pool, within 0.3 percentage
points of the reference run's offline selections, and the two top-2
memories agree on 91.7\% of episodes.

The throughput measurement uses batched serving with prefix caching
over 1,607 candidates after warm-up. SDA takes 4.1\,ms per candidate;
the importance report reaches 290 candidates/s at 3.4\,ms per candidate
(Table~\ref{tab:sdadeploy}).
